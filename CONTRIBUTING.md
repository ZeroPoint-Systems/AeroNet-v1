# Contributing / Working Rules

These 20 rules are non-negotiable for anyone (including a future version of you) working in this
repository. They exist to keep AeroNet's eventual claims defensible.

1. Every result must be traceable to raw data.
2. Every raw dataset must be traceable to an experiment configuration.
3. Every experiment must be reproducible from a recorded seed and configuration.
4. Every figure must be generated by code.
5. Every scientific claim must point to data, mathematics or literature.
6. Do not change the primary hypothesis because the first results are inconvenient.
7. Do not delete failed experiments.
8. Do not cherry-pick random seeds.
9. Do not give AeroNet information that the real decentralised system would not possess.
10. Keep simulation, hardware and analysis separate.
11. Keep baseline implementations separate from AeroNet.
12. Never mix raw and processed data.
13. Every experiment receives a unique ID.
14. Every code change receives version control.
15. Every hardware revision receives a hardware version.
16. Every scientific assumption is documented.
17. Every major claim has an associated falsification test.
18. Do not call something a phase transition until the data justify that terminology.
19. Do not claim novelty until the literature review supports the distinction.
20. Do not allow the competition deadline to determine the scientific conclusion.

## Definition of done

AeroNet is scientifically mature when every question below can be answered without improvising:

- What exactly is the research question?
- What exactly is the independent variable?
- What exactly is the dependent variable?
- What constitutes stable estimation?
- What constitutes unstable estimation?
- How is information quantified?
- How is the boundary detected?
- How is uncertainty in the boundary estimated?
- Why is this different from existing cooperative localisation work?
- Why is this different from communication-aware planning?
- Why is this different from information-driven exploration?
- Why is this different from collective perception?
- What happens if the boundary does not exist?
- What happens if the effect is caused only by connectivity?
- What happens if the effect disappears with realistic latency?
- What happens when an agent fails?
- What happens when sensing becomes biased?
- Does the effect scale with swarm size?
- Does it survive a different sensing modality?
- Does the adaptive controller genuinely shift the boundary?
- Can the simulation predict the physical experiment?
- Can another researcher reproduce the result?
- What exactly did [contributor] personally contribute?
- What are the limitations?
- What evidence would falsify the central claim?

If you cannot answer those, the project is not finished, regardless of how sophisticated the
drones look.
